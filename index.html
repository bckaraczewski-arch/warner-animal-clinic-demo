New clients

Welcome to the Practice

New to Lewisburg or switching veterinarians? Here is how to make your first visit simple.

Before your first visit

  • Bring any previous medical and vaccine records, or ask your last vet to send them to us.
  • Make a list of current medications, preventives, and diet.
  • Dogs on a leash and cats in a carrier, please.
  • For horses and livestock, call ahead so we can plan the visit. [Confirm farm call process]

We accept Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover, Diners Club, debit, cash, and check. Financing is available through CareCredit or Cherry. We do not offer in-house charging or credit accounts. [Confirm payment options and pet insurance]

What to expect

  1. A quick check-in and a review of your animal's history.
  2. A thorough exam tailored to your animal's age, species, and lifestyle.
  3. Clear recommendations, costs explained up front, and next steps.
  4. Time for your questions before you leave.

Request an Appointment

Request an appointment by email or phone and our front desk will get back to you to confirm a time. For sick or injured animals, please call the clinic directly.

How to request an appointment

Email: [Appointment email - pending]

Phone: (931) 359-3945

Please include your name, phone number, your animal's name and species, the reason for the visit, and your preferred days and times. A request is not a booked time until we confirm with you.

Sick or injured animal? Please call us at (931) 359-3945 right away. After hours, call Dr. Warner III at (931) 293-2719; if he cannot be reached, go to BluePearl Pet Hospital in Franklin, TN. See Emergency.

In an emergency

During clinic hours: call (931) 359-3945 on your way and we will tell you whether to come straight in.

After hours: call Dr. Warner III at his home office, (931) 293-2719.

If he cannot be reached in an emergency: go to BluePearl Pet Hospital in Franklin, TN.

Get help right away for: trouble breathing, injury or trauma, suspected poisoning, a swollen or bloated belly, repeated vomiting or diarrhea, colic signs in horses, or collapse. When in doubt, call.
